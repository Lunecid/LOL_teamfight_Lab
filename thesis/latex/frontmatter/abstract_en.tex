\begin{abstracten}
Engagements (teamfights) decide the course of a League of Legends (LoL) match, yet prior outcome-prediction work has either targeted whole-match victory or assumed sub-second replay observations that are available only through the game client. This thesis asks how far engagement outcomes can be predicted from the publicly available Riot Match-V5 telemetry alone, in which player state is sampled only every 60 seconds while events carry millisecond timestamps.

First, we propose a deterministic, kill-conditioned engagement localization algorithm: temporal clustering of kills (18 s), complete-linkage spatial diameter splitting (4,000 units), onset validation (at least two living players per team within 1,800 units), and merging of adjacent candidates (15 s / 2,000 units). Applied to \numMatches{} high-Elo ranked matches from the Korean server, it yields approximately one million engagements without manual annotation. Second, we define an exchange-value label that weights kills, shutdowns, objectives and bounties by domain value under softmax attention. Third, we construct a multi-modal representation---76 node, 26 global and 44 event features over six 5-second bins---under an explicit no-leakage contract that step-holds the 60-second frames and never consumes post-onset information. Fourth, we compare more than 25 models, including LightGBM, a matched-input multilayer perceptron, recurrent, attention, convolutional and state-space sequence models, graph neural networks, spatio-temporal graph networks and a layered gated-fusion model, under a common chronological patch hold-out (train 15.14 / validation 15.15 / test 15.16) with three seeds, bootstrap confidence intervals, DeLong tests and Holm--Bonferroni correction, and evaluate seven domain-knowledge treatments with a five-phase ablation protocol.

LightGBM on engineered tabular summaries achieves the best test AUC of \aucLGBM{}; the matched-input MLP reaches \aucMLP{}, and sequence, graph and event-based neural representations cluster at \aucNeuralLow{}--\aucNeuralHigh{}. The matched-input comparison attributes about half of the gap to the learner family and half to the input representation. Predictability rises monotonically with game phase (early \aucEarly{}, mid \aucMid{}, late \aucLate{}) and with pre-onset gold imbalance (close \aucClose{}, moderate \aucModerate{}, one-sided \aucOneSided{}), approaching chance for close engagements. These results indicate an information-granularity ceiling imposed by minute-resolution public telemetry. We also present a human-annotation validation protocol for the detector and a code-versus-paper audit, and release the code under the MIT license.
\end{abstracten}
