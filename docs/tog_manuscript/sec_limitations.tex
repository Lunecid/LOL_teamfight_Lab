\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limitations}

This section states what the study does not establish. Each limitation is given with the
measurement that bounds it; where that measurement does not exist yet, the text says so.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{subsec:limitations}

\subsubsection{What a kill-anchored definition cannot see}

The detector starts from champion kills. An exchange in which both teams commit and then
disengage without a kill (zoning, an abandoned objective contest, or an exchange of abilities
followed by a retreat) is therefore not an instance (\cref{sec:def-killless}). To bound how often
such exchanges occur, we scanned a seeded sample of 20,000 matches for proximity encounters. The
sample is drawn from the whole 210,000-match cache, not only from the 191,940 matches that yield
labelled engagements, and all of it falls in patches 15.14--15.16.
% FEAT/tog_revision/killless_grid/summary.json rows[*].matches 20000, seed 7 (written 2026-09-11 11:25:37,
% git_commit 5600f8b; _progress.txt "ALL DONE" at 11:25:18).  Sample drawn from the cache, not the labelled
% corpus: REPO/scripts/run_killless_encounters.py main() (sorted CACHE_DIR ids, random.Random(seed).sample).
% 210,000 = count of CACHE/*.meta.json.  Patch mix recomputed 2026-09-11:
% random.Random(7).sample(sorted cache ids, 20000) -> meta "patch" 15.14: 7190, 15.15: 7121, 15.16: 5689 (sum 20000).
% 191,940: FEAT/scale_decomposition_v33_market_event.json n_matches.
A proximity encounter is a run of consecutive points of the 5\,s position grid at which some alive
champion has at least $k$ alive champions of each team, itself included, within $R$ of its position.
It counts as containing a kill if a champion kill falls inside the run or within 15\,s of either end.
% REPO/scripts/run_killless_encounters.py encounters_for_match(): every alive champion is tried as the anchor and
% counts for its own team (distance 0); has_kill = any kill_ts in [t0 - grace_ms, t1 + grace_ms];
% grace_ms 15000: summary.json rows[*].grace_ms.
With $R = 1{,}600$\,u and $k = 4$, 351 of 12,602 encounters (2.79\%) contain no kill when the minimum
duration is 20\,s (four grid points), and 591 of 19,155 (3.09\%) when it is $G$ (13.7\,s, which the
scanner rounds to three grid points).
% summary.json rows[setting=r1600_t4_d20_g15]: encounters 12602, killless 351, killless_share_of_encounters
% 0.027853 (= 351 / 12602); rows[setting=r1600_t4_dG_g15]: encounters 19155, killless 591, share 0.030854 (= 591 / 19155).
% Grid points: encounters_for_match() min_frames = max(1, round(min_duration_s * 1000 / 5000)): 20 s -> 4;
% 13.7 s -> round(2.74) = 3.
These are 0.018 and 0.030 kill-less encounters per match, against 0.572 engagements per match in the
teamfight class (at least four participants on each side) of the labelled corpus.
% killless_per_match 0.01755 (= 351 / 20000) and 0.02955 (= 591 / 20000): same rows;
% corpus_reference.teamfight_class_per_match 0.57220 (= 109829 / 191940; teamfight n from
% FEAT/scale_decomposition_v33_market_event.json by_participation_scale.teamfight.n).
Lowering $k$ raises the share: at $k = 3$ it is 3.27\% (20\,s) and 3.99\% ($G$), and at $k = 2$ with
the minimum set to $G$ it is 7.95\% (16,756 of 210,738). At $k = 4$ and $G$, the share is 2.00\% for
$R = 1{,}200$\,u, 3.47\% for $R = 2{,}000$\,u, and 4.34\% with a 10\,s grace period.
% summary.json killless_share_of_encounters: rows[r1600_t3_d20_g15] 0.032722; rows[r1600_t3_dG_g15] 0.039922;
% rows[r1600_t2_dG_g15] 0.079511 (= 16756 / 210738); rows[r1200_t4_dG_g15] 0.020003; rows[r2000_t4_dG_g15] 0.034711;
% rows[r1600_t4_dG_g10] 0.043435.  k = 2 at 20 s (rows[r1600_t2_d20_g15] 0.067818) is also above k = 3.
Three cautions govern these numbers. Their denominator is proximity encounters, not engagements.
Proximity is not commitment: champions standing near one another need not be fighting, and a fight
conducted at longer range need not register as an encounter, so the per-match rates cannot be
divided into engagement counts to estimate how many fights the corpus omits. Finally, the grid does
not locate the two kinds of encounter equally well. For every kill it moves the killer, the victim
and the assisting champions along a straight line from their positions in the preceding frame to
the kill position, whereas champions in a kill-less encounter keep positions interpolated between
frames 60\,s apart, and alive status is held from the most recent frame.
% summary.json denominator_warning.  Grid: REPO/gameplay/fight_clustering.py build_5s_position_grid() (linear
% between minute frames; kill participants re-placed on a line from the preceding frame to the kill position
% when TF2_USE_KILL_TRAJECTORY_INTERP); alive held: REPO/scripts/run_killless_encounters.py main()
% (searchsorted on minute_ts, side="right").
The share depends strongly on how an encounter is defined: in Dota~2, Schubert et al.\ found no
kill in 81.1\% of the encounters returned by their proximity-based detector~\cite{schubert2016encounter}.
% 81.1%: REPO/docs/DEFINITION_LITERATURE.md (Schubert 2016 entry, PDF read 2026-09-07); the 2026-09-11 fact-check
% found it on PDF p. 14 as 18,744 of 23,110 encounters without kill events (18744 / 23110 = 0.8111).

Frequency is only half of the question, because a fight can be won without a kill. Under the
market label such a win is not mis-measured but unmeasured. The label sums, over events inside
the attribution disc, the gold each kill paid (bounty and shutdown bounty read from the event,
plus the fitted 20\,g per kill and 45\,g per assist) and the fitted price of each plate, turret,
inhibitor and epic monster.
% REPO/gameplay/labels.py _compute_label_market_event() (kill gold = bounty + shutdownBounty + table kills + table
% assists x assisting ids) and _priced_event_gold(); kills 20.0, assists 45.0: REPO/config/game_rules/event_prices.json pooled.
A kill-less exchange that ends in a retreat pays no gold and writes no event, so its swing is
zero and falls inside the $\pm 300$\,g dead zone. The refinement that follows finds no kill
difference, then compares the number of living champions on each team, then the sign of
structure, plate and monster events, and otherwise declares a draw.
% dead zone 300 g: SHARDS/manifest.json label.gold_deadzone 300.0; refinement order: REPO/gameplay/labels.py
% _lex_refine() (cluster kills -> alive counts -> sign of ELITE_MONSTER_KILL / BUILDING_KILL /
% TURRET_PLATE_DESTROYED -> tie policy).
The living-champion count covers all ten champions, not only the participants. A kill-less fight
would therefore be decided by a death elsewhere on the map, by a structure, plate or monster event
inside the disc, or by the tie policy (a seeded coin flip for the stored row label, exclusion for
every reported label variant), but not by the fight itself.
% all ten champions: REPO/gameplay/labels.py _lex_refine() (alive summed over every champion of each team, read by
% interp_node_global at the last kill or the window end); tie policies: SHARDS/manifest.json
% label.row_tie_policy "random", label.extra_tie_policy "drop".
Measuring kill-less fights would require a second label, not only a second detector.

Instances are also constructed retrospectively. The cutoff is placed 15\,s before the first
kill, a kill that has not happened at prediction time.
% 15 s: SHARDS/manifest.json detector.TF2_ENGAGE_PRE_KILL_MS = 15000.
The task is therefore to predict which side wins an engagement given that one is about to
occur at that place, not to anticipate whether one will occur; the second task needs a trigger
that does not depend on the future.

\subsubsection{What the label measures}

The market label prices non-kill events with a table fitted by ordinary least squares (OLS):
each team's gold change over a one-minute frame interval is regressed on that team's event
counts in the interval, pooled over 24,000 matches (8,000 per patch, 1,173,822 intervals,
$R^2 = 0.962$).
% FEAT/event_prices.json fits.pooled: n_matches 24000, n_intervals 1173822, r2 0.96182;
% n_matches_per_patch 8000, seed 7.
A coefficient is the average team gold associated with an event conditional on the other
regressors. It is not the payout written in the game rules, and it absorbs income that
co-occurs with the event in the same minute.
% REPO/analysis/event_prices.py module docstring.
Four entries show what this means in practice. We report them rather than adjust them.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Elemental dragon.} The fitted coefficient is $-41.83$\,g (95\% interval
  $[-43.12, -40.59]$\,g from 200 cluster-bootstrap resamples of 23,829 matches). The table
  construction assumes that the frames count an epic monster as one jungle creep, adds that creep's
  fitted price (23.33\,g) back, and clamps the result ($-18.50$\,g) at zero. An elemental dragon
  therefore moves the gold swing by 0\,g and can influence a verdict only through the dead-zone
  refinement.

  % [-43.12328, -40.58769]: fits.pooled.cluster_bootstrap.dragon; -41.82868 + 23.33189 = -18.49679;
  % "cluster bootstrap over 23,829 matches x 200": FEAT/event_prices_boot.log; add-back and clamp:
  % REPO/analysis/event_prices.py price_table(); table value 0.0: REPO/config/game_rules/event_prices.json pooled.dragon.
  Dragons are not rare near engagements: in the 26,712-engagement sample of the win-probability
  study, 1,060 engagements contain an elemental dragon kill between the pre-fight query point and

\item \emph{Turrets.} An inner turret is priced at 730\,g (fitted 729.79\,g, interval

  25\,g for an inner turret, with no local gold~\pending{cite}{League of Legends Wiki, ``Turret''
  page, accessed 11 Sep.\ 2026}. The page gives current values, which we could not confirm for
  15.14--15.16 (\pending{rulefact}{patch-dated turret rewards for 15.14--15.16}).
  % Wiki Turret page read 2026-09-11 (local 0, outer and nexus 50 global, inner and base 25 global, first-turret

\item \emph{First turret.} The first-turret coefficient is negative, $-53.44$\,g (interval

entry they contain, and the Elder Dragon is absent from all of them because each patch has fewer
than the 200 events the construction requires.

% REPO/analysis/event_prices.py price_table(min_count=200.0); the label loads "pooled":
% REPO/gameplay/labels.py _event_price_table().
The table is also fitted on matches from the same corpus that is later evaluated. The prices
are label constants, not model inputs, so this is not a feature leak, but the label is not
independent of the corpus.

The label reads only events whose position lies within $D = 4{,}264$\,u of the first kill.
% radius = CLUSTER_MAX_DIAMETER when LABEL_ATTRIBUTION_RADIUS_U = 0 (default 0.0 in REPO/core/config.py):
% REPO/gameplay/labels.py attribute_events(); anchor = first kill position: REPO/data/index_split.py _fight_anchor(),
% REPO/core/fight_types.py FightRef.anchor_x ("fight centre (first kill position)");
% D = 4264.0: SHARDS/manifest.json detector.CLUSTER_MAX_DIAMETER.
Three properties of this rule are conventions rather than measurements. First, $D$ bounds the
diameter of a kill cluster but is used here as a radius, so the disc is 8,528\,u across, twice
the widest cluster the detector accepts. Second, the disc stays at the first kill and does not
follow a fight that moves. Third, the disc is isotropic, whereas the champion-sharing crossover
that defines $D$ is longer along a lane than across it (5,212\,u along the top lane against
3,456\,u across it).
% 2 x 4264 = 8528.  FEAT/fight_boundary_full/details_pooled.json spatial.lane_anisotropy.top:
% along.crossover_u 5212.1, across.crossover_u 3456.1.
Events without a position, such as ward kills, are never attributed. In a 553-match pilot
(1,438 engagements), 39.1\% of engagements had at least one event outside the disc within the
label window; attribution changed the verdict of 3.0\% of the engagements labelled under both
rules and changed whether 2.6\% were draws. No radius other than $D$ has been evaluated.
% FEAT/label_window_fixes.json attribution: has_far_events_share 0.3908,
% flip_share_among_both_labelled 0.0301, draw_status_changed_share 0.0257; n_matches 553,
% n_engagements 1438.  Positionless events dropped: REPO/gameplay/labels.py attribute_events()

\subsubsection{What the inputs observe}

Champion state (position, gold, level, items) arrives in frames 60\,s apart, whereas kills,
purchases and objectives are timestamped to the millisecond.
% frame spacing 60000-60003 ms: CACHE/<one match drawn from a seeded 300-match sample>.npz minute_ts
% (one match checked; the final partial frame at game end is shorter).
At the cutoff, the most recent frame is a median 33.6\,s old (10th and 90th percentiles 8.2\,s
and 51.6\,s, 553-match pilot).
% FEAT/prediction_situation_pilot.json checks.freshness.age_s_quantiles p10 8.18 / p50 33.60 / p90 51.55.
Holding the last frame misplaces a champion. Over 152,517 kills in 3,000 matches, the last-frame
position of the victim lies a median 2,126\,u from where it died (90th percentile 6,779\,u),
rising to 3,746\,u when the frame is 50--60\,s old.
% FEAT/fight_boundary/interp_and_horizon.json: n_matches 3000, n_kills_measured 152517,
% interp_error_u.hold p50 2126 / p90 6779; interp_error_by_since_frame_s["50-60"].hold 3746.
Linear interpolation towards the next frame roughly halves the median error, to 1,149\,u, but it
uses information from after the cutoff, so it is used only to construct instances, never as a
model input. Even so, the interpolated victim position lies within $R = 1{,}600$\,u of the kill
site for only 61.7\% of kills. The presence gate, which compares positions against $R$, therefore
inherits a position error of the same order as $R$ itself.
% interp_error_u.linear p50 1149; linear_share_within["1600"] 0.617: same file.
% feature path holds, gate interpolates: REPO/docs/DEFINITION_EVIDENCE.md section 18.1 (table of interpolation paths:
% features, presence gate, label gold).
The observation-bound analysis quantifies how much of the prediction ceiling this gap explains.
Here we note only that it also limits the definition.

The observation window, 30\,s before the cutoff summarised in six 5\,s bins, is carried over
from the CoG submission; it was not derived.
% FIGHT_CONTEXT_SEC 30 and BIN_MS 5000: REPO/core/config.py (30 / 5 = 6 bins); "not derived from data, a CoG
% convention": REPO/docs/DEFINITION_EVIDENCE.md section 18.1; kept on 2026-09-09: REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md
% section 7 (decision log).
Because frames are 60\,s apart, a 30\,s window contains at most one frame, so its length mostly
changes how many events are seen rather than how much state. The only measurement so far is a
553-match pilot that predates the input-audit leak fixes and is therefore not a v3.3 result: it used
1,360 labelled rows from 1,438 engagements, the \texttt{market\_lex} label, and 7,105 pre-fix feature
columns without the frame-age column. It gives AUCs of 0.614, 0.626 and 0.631 for windows of 15, 30
and 60\,s, without intervals, and we draw no conclusion from that ordering for the v3.3 features.
% FEAT/prediction_situation_pilot.json checks.ctx: 15 -> 0.61420, 30 -> 0.62626, 60 -> 0.63127; n_matches 553,
% n_refs 1438, n_rows 1360, n_features 7105, v3.LABEL_TYPE market_lex.  Pre-fix status: REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md
% section 5.4 "pilot and audit metrics" (second 5.4), footnote 1: pilot written 2026-09-09 05:33, before commits
% 5b5f9c8 and 86095c8, no frame_age_s, "not v3.3 results".
The full-corpus sweep is \pending{window}{full-corpus AUC for observation windows of 15, 30, 60 and
120\,s and bin widths of 2.5, 5 and 10\,s under the v3.3 preset}. Until it is reported, 30\,s is a
convention.

The win-probability model that values an engagement is least reliable late in the game. Even
after sampling one training point per 5-minute bucket, only 2,517 of its 40,228 fitting rows
(6.3\%) and 176 of its 4,986 test rows fall after 30 minutes.
% WT/outputs/temporal_winprob_v3_buckets/sampling_census.json: fit.rows 40228, fit.bands["30-1000"] 2517;
% test.rows 4986, test.bands["30-1000"] 176; 2517 / 40228 = 0.0626.
At the engagement cutoff, its expected calibration error (ECE) is 0.069 after 30 minutes (1,077
engagements from 817 matches), against 0.009, 0.024 and 0.012 in the 2--10, 10--20 and 20--30
minute bands, while its AUC in the late band is 0.844.
% WT/outputs/temporal_winprob_v3_buckets/validation_a/results.json
% engagement_all_available.pre.families.expanded.by_game_time_min: 30+ ece 0.06902, n 1077,
% matches 817, auc 0.84433; 2-10 ece 0.00906; 10-20 ece 0.02431; 20-30 ece 0.01188.
Value swings of late engagements are therefore measured with the least calibrated instrument in
the study.

\subsubsection{What the corpus covers}

Every one of the 210,000 match records in the corpus cache carries the Korean platform prefix,
and the labelled corpus comes from three patches, with 187,547, 191,184 and 153,816 engagements in
15.14, 15.15 and 15.16.
% CACHE/: 210000 *.meta.json files, all prefixed "KR_"; patch counts:
% FEAT/scale_decomposition_v33_market_event.json patch_distribution.
The learner comparison tests transfer to one adjacent patch only. Nothing here speaks to other
regions, later patches, lower ranks or professional play.

Rank is a property of how the matches were collected, not a measured variable. The collection
targeted Master tier and above, but the cached records carry no rank, tier or league-point
field, so results cannot be conditioned or stratified on skill.
% collection frame: REPO/analysis/analysis.py summary-table entry sampling_frame "KR Master+ ladder (collection
% constraint; per-match tier not retained)"; tier claim withdrawn in commit 5eaf4ea.  No tier field: CACHE/*.meta.json
% keys (match_id, patch_full, patch, team_map, role_slots, anchors, anchor_is_norm, static_meta, interp_policy,
% feature_version), 0 of 300 seeded samples contain a tier or league-point key.

The cache carries no player identity. Participants are identified by slot, team and champion; in
a seeded sample of 300 matches, neither the match metadata nor the event files contain the Riot
player identifier (PUUID).
% CACHE/: 0 of 300 sampled *.meta.json and 0 of 300 *.events.json contain "puuid".
Player-level questions, such as individual skill, familiar duos, or a player on both sides of a
fold, cannot be asked. Match-grouped cross-validation cannot group by player either, so the same
player may appear in training and evaluation folds; the size of that effect is unknown.

\subsubsection{What the learner comparison establishes}

The deep tabular models were trained with one fixed configuration and no per-architecture
search. The token-based models, FT-Transformer~\cite{gorishniy2021revisiting} and
SAINT~\cite{somepalli2021saint}, used token dimension 32, three layers, eight attention heads,
batch size 64 and learning rate $10^{-4}$.
% FEAT/deep_tabular_v33_patch_ft.json and deep_tabular_v33_patch_saint.json token_config {d_token 32, n_layers 3,
% n_heads 8, batch_size 64, lr 0.0001}; no per-architecture search: REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md section 5.4
% "learner comparison" (first 5.4), notes and limitation paragraph.
The neural learners are not matched in capacity either: the multilayer perceptron (MLP) has
4.17\,M trainable parameters and TabNet~\cite{arik2021tabnet} 4.08\,M, against 0.46\,M for SAINT
and 0.29\,M for FT-Transformer.

\subsubsection{How precisely the constants are known}

$R = 1{,}600$\,u and $B = 15$\,s are read from the game rules, not estimated: $R$ is the radius
within which a champion's death shares experience, and $B$ is the window within which the game
credits a kill or an assist on Summoner's Rift. Minute frames cannot resolve either quantity.
% SHARDS/manifest.json detector.TF2_VALIDITY_RADIUS 1600.0, TF2_ENGAGE_PRE_KILL_MS 15000;
% FEAT/fight_boundary_full/spec_pooled.json radius_source "rule:champion-death experience share radius",
% lead_source "rule:kill/assist credit window (Summoner's Rift)".
Both values were taken from League of Legends Wiki pages read on 8 September 2026 and re-read on
11 September 2026~\pending{cite}{League of Legends Wiki, ``Experience (champion)'', ``Assist'' and
``Kill'' pages, accessed 11 Sep.\ 2026}, and were applied unchanged to all three patches.
% first read: REPO/docs/DEFINITION_EVIDENCE.md section 15 (game-rule constants table, 2026-09-08); re-read:
% REPO/docs/references/rule_constants_evidence.md section 1 (retrieved 2026-09-11); Experience (champion) page
% fetched again for this section on 2026-09-11: 1,600 u for champion deaths, 1,500 u for minions.
% Same R, B per patch: FEAT/fight_boundary_full/spec_15.14.json, spec_15.15.json, spec_15.16.json.
The pages give current values without dating them. The patch history of the experience page lists
no change to the champion-death radius; its range entries concern minion experience only. The
official notes for the three corpus patches list no change to either rule~\pending{cite}{Riot Games,
patch 25.14, 25.15 and 25.16 notes}. That no note lists a change does not show that a rule was
unchanged: notes can omit changes, and hotfixes announced elsewhere were not searched.

% 1,250 -> 1,400, no champion-death radius entry (WebFetch 2026-09-11; also rule_constants_evidence.md section 1).
% Assist page: no patch-history entries; Kill page: none for the 15 s window (rule_constants_evidence.md section 1;
% Kill page re-fetched 2026-09-11).  Notes 25.14-25.16: "no change listed" for the experience radius and the assist
% window (rule_constants_evidence.md section 2, automated-fetch reading; that file asks for a manual check).

The two kinds of constant have been probed with different measures, so their effects cannot be
ranked against each other. For the rule anchors, on 2,971 matches (81,914 kill episodes) the share
of episodes that pass the presence gate is 10.3\% at the operating point and ranges from 6.5\% to
41.1\% over $R \in \{1{,}350, 1{,}600, 1{,}800, 2{,}500\}$\,u and $B \in \{5, 10, 15\}$\,s.
% FEAT/fight_boundary/presence_sensitivity.json: n_matches 2971, n_episodes 81914, constants.R_grid, B_grid_ms;
% grid.R1600_B15.valid_share 0.1027, minimum grid.R1350_B15 0.0649, maximum grid.R2500_B5 0.4112.
For $G$, every swept value from 10 to 18\,s partitions kills with an adjusted Rand index (ARI) of at
least 0.90 against the valley partition; this measures agreement between partitions, not the size of
the corpus. How $G$ and $D$ together change the corpus is \pending{gd}{engagement count and AUC over
the $G \times D$ sweep under the v3.3 preset}.
% FEAT/fight_boundary_full/details_pooled.json temporal.ari_vs_valley: 10 -> 0.9048, 12 -> 0.9609,
% 13 -> 0.9844, 14 -> 0.9942, 15 -> 0.9746, 16 -> 0.9562, 18 -> 0.9225 (8 -> 0.8293 and 20 -> 0.8916 fall below).

The derived constants carry numerical limits of their own. $G$ is the minimum of a kernel density
estimate (KDE) evaluated on 1,401 points spaced uniformly in the base-10 logarithm of the interval
from $10^{-0.5}$ to $10^{3}$\,s. The step is 0.0025 decades, or 79\,ms at 13.72\,s.
% grid (-0.5, 3.0, 1401): REPO/analysis/temporal_boundary.py kde_valley() default, evaluated with np.linspace(*grid);
% step 3.5 / 1400 = 0.0025; 13.7246 * (10**0.0025 - 1) = 0.0792 s.
The pooled valley and all three per-patch valleys fall on grid points, and the pooled and 15.16
estimates are the same point (13.7246\,s), so differences below one step cannot be resolved. The
detector was run with $G = 13.700$\,s, 25\,ms from the estimate and inside the match-bootstrap
interval $[12.16, 15.67]$\,s.
% gap_s 13.724609610075618 in both FEAT/fight_boundary_full/spec_pooled.json and spec_15.16.json;
% 15.14 13.9637 and 15.15 13.4896 also on grid nodes (checked); gap_ci_s [12.160, 15.668]:
% spec_pooled.json; detector 13700 ms: SHARDS/manifest.json; 13.7246 - 13.700 = 0.0246 s.
$D$ is the distance at which the champion-sharing rate crosses 50\%, found by linear interpolation
between the centres of the 3,750\,u and 4,500\,u distance bins (rates 0.647 and 0.432). Its
match-bootstrap interval, $[4{,}256, 4{,}274]$\,u, is only 18\,u wide because it resamples matches
with the bins fixed; it does not include the uncertainty of placing a crossing inside a 750\,u gap.
The detector was run with $D = 4{,}264.0$\,u.
% linear interpolation between bin centres: REPO/analysis/spatial_boundary.py crossover() docstring;
% FEAT/fight_boundary_full/details_pooled.json spatial.curve [3750, 0.6469], [4500, 0.4325];
% 3750 + (0.6469 - 0.5) / (0.6469 - 0.4325) * 750 = 4263.9; crossover 4263.87, bootstrap [4256.05, 4273.97]
% (width 17.92): spec_pooled.json; detector 4264.0: SHARDS/manifest.json.
Both constants, like the price table, are estimated on the corpus that is later evaluated
(208,141 matches).

60\,s duration cap, and truncation when one team is eliminated) are carried over from the
released CoG code, and no sensitivity analysis is reported for them.

\subsection{Future Work}\label{subsec:future-work}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Measurement from replays.} Replay video can measure what this study had to assume: the
  time from first contact to first kill (for $B$), the distance of participants at the first kill
  (for $R$), the error of holding the last frame, and the kill-less encounters that the detector
  cannot see. The practical obstacle is that the 1,053 replay files available can be played only
  on patch 16.15, not on the corpus patches.
  % REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md section 8 item 7; replay files: REPO/docs/DATA_MANIFEST.md corpus table,
  % row "Replays 16.15" (1,053 ROFL, playable only on 16.15).
\item \emph{Channel ablation.} An ablation over the four input channels (fixed match information,
  frame snapshots, millisecond events and terrain), with each channel alone, each left out, and all
  added cumulatively, would show which channel carries the signal the learners use.
  % REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md section 8 item 2.
\item \emph{Per-patch rule constants.} A per-patch table of rule constants (experience-sharing
  radius, credit window and event prices) would replace the pooled values assumed here and make the
  definition portable across patches, in line with patch-agnostic analytics built on game-design
  parameters~\cite{chitayat2023beyond}.
  % REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md section 8 item 5.
\item \emph{Data cache.} A rebuilt cache would drop the six champion-statistic fields that the
  application programming interface (API) always reports as zero, and would evaluate adding the
  \texttt{goldPerSecond} frame field, which the current features do not use.
  % REPO/docs/RELEASE_REPORT_V3.md section 8 item 6 (remove the dead stats; review goldPerSecond,
  % victimTeamfightDamage and FEAT_UPDATE) and section 6 (six dead API fields, goldPerSecond unused);
  % six zero-valued stats: REPO/docs/DEFINITION_EVIDENCE.md section 20.1.
\item \emph{A label and a detector for kill-less fights.} Scoring position and objective control
  rather than paid gold, triggered by proximity rather than by a kill, would address the blind spot
  measured above. Such a detector would also need positions of the same accuracy for encounters with
  and without kills.
\item \emph{Online anticipation.} Live coaching or broadcast use requires a trigger computed from
  pre-fight state only, in place of the retrospective cutoff, together with a latency budget; live
  win prediction in professional play shows the form such a system takes~\cite{hodge2021win}.
  % CoG future work "Real-time deployment": REPO/docs/CoG2026_Paper.md section 8.3.
\item \emph{Other regions and professional play.} Re-running the definition pipeline on other
  servers would test whether $G$ and $D$ are properties of the game or of the Korean high-rank
  population. Professional matches, with far fewer games per patch, would test whether the
  estimates survive small samples.
  % REPO/docs/CoG2026_Paper.md section 8.3 ("Cross-region transfer", "Professional match adaptation").
\item \emph{Causal analysis.} The value-swing analysis is associational. Estimating the effect of
  winning an engagement on winning the match requires a causal design that accounts for the state
  that made the engagement likely.
  % REPO/docs/CoG2026_Paper.md section 8.3 ("Causal analysis").
\item \emph{Audience-facing tools.} Encounter detection was introduced as a basis for esports
  analytics~\cite{schubert2016encounter}. Research on esports audiences has built data-driven content
  for broadcasts~\cite{block2018narrative}, data-driven audience experiences~\cite{kokkinakis2020dax},
  real-time dashboards for spectators~\cite{charleer2018dashboards}, interactive audience
  experiences~\cite{pedrassoli2024passive} and win-condition narratives~\cite{pedrassoli2024wincondition}.
  An engagement record (start, end, participants, scale class, pre-fight probability and realised
  value swing) is a structured game event that such tools could consume. A broadcast tool could flag
  engagements won by the side the model gave a low probability, or list the engagements that moved
  win probability most. With a pre-fight AUC of 0.670, the probability is a coarse cue, not a
  forecast to announce, and whether such annotations help viewers needs a user study, which we have
  not run.
  % keys block2018narrative, kokkinakis2020dax, charleer2018dashboards, pedrassoli2024passive,
  % pedrassoli2024wincondition: REPO/docs/references/definition_refs.bib section 3, each [VERIFIED 2026-09-11].
  % 0.670: FEAT/scale_decomposition_v33_market_event.json overall_auc 0.66991.
\end{itemize}
