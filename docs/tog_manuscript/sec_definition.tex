\section{Defining the Engagement}\label{sec:definition}

The conference version of this study chained kills that were at most 18\,s apart and split them when
they were more than 4,000 game units (u) apart. Both values were set by hand, and reviewers rightly
asked why. This section replaces them with a rule whose two boundary constants are estimated from the
corpus, whose two gate constants are taken from rules the game itself applies, and whose remaining
constants are listed with the evidence behind each, or with the statement that none has been measured
(Table~\ref{tab:constants}). It ends with what a kill-anchored definition cannot observe
(Section~\ref{sec:def-killless}).

\subsection{Construct and operationalisation}

``Teamfight'' is a player term, not a measurement unit. We found no formal definition from the
publisher; the community wiki's glossary describes a teamfight informally as several champions,
possibly whole teams, meeting in one place to fight~\cite{lolwiki2026terminology}.
% src: lolwiki2026terminology (Terminology, revision 4057849 of 2026-08-28, entry "Teamfight" in section T);
%   paraphrase checked against the entry by item W1b (references_audit.md 2c, row sec_definition.tex:27).
The formal definitions that exist disagree on what makes a fight. The \emph{encounter} of Schubert et
al., defined for Dota~2, begins when heroes (Dota~2's counterpart of champions) of opposing teams come
within combat range of each other~\cite{schubert2016encounter}.
The \emph{team fight} of Ke et al.\ is an encounter in which one side has at least two players and at
least one kill occurs~\cite{ke2022}. The OpenDota labels used by Tot et al.~\cite{tot2021camera} need
only deaths: consecutive hero deaths less than 15\,s apart are grouped, groups of at least three are
kept, and no spatial condition is applied~\cite{opendota2018processteamfights}. The definitions thus
differ in whether proximity, a kill or several deaths define a fight, and in how many champions must
take part.
% src: D:/LOL_Project/references/Schubert2016_EncounterDetection_SSAC.pdf (pdftotext, 2026-09-14): an encounter is established when two or
%   more heroes of opposing teams are within combat range (section 4); D:/LOL_Project/references/Ke2022_TeamFightModels_CoG.pdf section
%   VI-B "Team Fight Definition": an encounter with one side having at least two players of the same team and at least one killing event;
%   OpenDota: odota/core processors/processTeamfights.js at e2e4432 (a death opens a fight if none is open; the fight closes at the first
%   interval event 15 or more after its last death; filter deaths >= 3; no position test) -- references_audit.md 3.2.
We therefore treat the engagement as an operationalised construct in the sense of measurement modelling
\cite{jacobs2021measurement}: a rule that maps observable telemetry to instances of an unobservable
concept, whose assumptions are stated and whose reliability and validity are tested rather than
asserted.

\subsection{Kill episodes and the temporal boundary \texorpdfstring{$G$}{G}}

Riot's Match-V5 timeline~\cite{riotapi2024timeline} has no event for damage exchanged between champions.
Damage and health appear only in the participant frames, which are written once a minute, and as a
summary of the damage behind each death attached to the kill event. The only champion-versus-champion
combat event that the timeline timestamps to the millisecond and places on the map is the champion
kill.
% src: cached timeline of KR_7715477686 (D:/LOL_Project/cache/match_cache_fresh_v3_engage_status13): event types
%   BUILDING_KILL, CHAMPION_KILL, CHAMPION_SPECIAL_KILL, DRAGON_SOUL_GIVEN, ELITE_MONSTER_KILL, FEAT_UPDATE, GAME_END,
%   ITEM_*, LEVEL_UP, PAUSE_END, SKILL_LEVEL_UP, TURRET_PLATE_DESTROYED, WARD_KILL, WARD_PLACED (no damage event);
%   CHAMPION_KILL keys include victimDamageDealt, victimDamageReceived, position, timestamp; minute-frame node
%   columns ds_totalDamageDoneToChampions, hp_pct (core/contract.py NODE_IDX).  Checked 2026-09-14.
The unit is therefore built from kills, and the population is the set of combat episodes that resolve
with at least one death. This is a \emph{case definition} in the epidemiological sense, a rule that
fixes which instances are counted: fights without a kill exist, but they lie outside the observable
population.

Consecutive kills of a match are chained into an episode when their gap is at most $G$. Intervals
between kills of the same fight are short and intervals between fights are long, so the distribution of
all intervals has two peaks, and the natural boundary is the lowest point between them, its
\emph{antimode}. Session identification from inter-activity times \cite{halfaker2015session,mehrzadi2012session}
and earthquake declustering from nearest-neighbour space--time distances \cite{zaliapin2013clusters}
place their boundaries the same way, and a kernel density estimate (KDE), a smoothed histogram, is the
standard tool for locating such peaks \cite{silverman1981multimodality}. On a base-10 logarithmic scale,
the intervals between consecutive kills have a within-fight mode at 5.7\,s and a between-fight mode at
62.7\,s.
% src: FB/details_pooled.json temporal.kde.mode1_s = 5.7214, mode2_s = 62.7336
A two-component Gaussian mixture decides on which side of the distribution each mode lies, and the
boundary is the minimum of a Gaussian KDE between the two KDE peaks (analysis/temporal\_boundary.py,
\texttt{kde\_valley}). Halfaker et al.\ use the mixture's posterior crossing instead. Mehrzadi and
Feitelson warn that a global threshold can leave artefacts of its own value in the extracted sessions,
which is one reason to derive the threshold from the data and to re-derive it for every patch.

Across the corpus of 208,141 matches (10,417,458 intervals; the KDE and the mixture are fitted to a
seeded subsample of 1,000,000 of them) the valley lies at $\hat G = 13.7246$\,s. Its depth, the KDE
density at the valley divided by the lower of the two mode densities, is 0.87.
% src: FB/spec_pooled.json n_matches 208141, n_intervals 10417458, gap_s 13.724609610075618, gap_depth 0.8660
% src: analysis/temporal_boundary.py MAX_POINTS = 1_000_000 (kde_valley and mixture_crossing subsample with seed);

confidence interval (CI) of 12.2--15.7\,s. Each replicate is subsampled to 100,000 intervals, so this
interval is wider than the full corpus warrants.

14.1\,s; the mixture crossing lies at 14.5\,s; and the three patches give 14.0, 13.5 and 13.7\,s.

% src: FB/drift.json patches.*.gap_s 13.9637 / 13.4896 / 13.7246
In a 9,903-match pilot, the terciles of match kill count gave 11.4, 14.0 and 14.5\,s.

\emph{Reported precision.} The KDE is evaluated on a fixed grid of 1,401 nodes spanning
$\log_{10}$ intervals from $-0.5$ to $3.0$, a step of 0.0025 in $\log_{10}$, or 79\,ms at 13.7\,s.
% src: analysis/temporal_boundary.py kde_valley(grid=(-0.5, 3.0, 1401)); python: g=np.linspace(-0.5,3.0,1401) -> node 655 = 13.72461 s, neighbours 13.64583 s and 13.80384 s (steps 78.8 ms and 79.2 ms)
The valley is therefore always a grid node. This is why the pooled estimate and the estimate for patch
15.16 coincide to every printed digit (13.7246\,s), and why the bandwidths 0.12 and 0.16 return the
same valley (13.5675\,s): the coincidence reflects the grid, not unusual stability. We report $G$ to one
decimal place. The detector was run with $G = 13.700$\,s, the estimate $\hat G = 13.7246$\,s rounded
before it was injected; the rounding moves $G$ by 25\,ms, less than one grid step.

% src: SHARDS/manifest.json detector.TF2_KILL_CLUSTER_GAP_MS 13700 (= effective_overrides); core/presets.py PRESETS["v3.3"]
% cmd: 13.7246 - 13.700 = 0.0246 s

\emph{Plateau and continuity with the conference version.} The adjusted Rand index (ARI) measures how
similarly two partitions group the same items: it is 1 for identical groupings and close to 0 for
agreement no better than chance \cite{hubert1985comparing}. The partition is insensitive to $G$ near the
valley: the ARI between the temporal episodes at $\hat G$ and those at another gap stays at or above 0.9
for every tested gap from 10 to 18\,s, which we report as the plateau, in the spirit of stability-based
cluster selection \cite{campello2013hdbscan}. The hand-set 18\,s of the conference version, which this
paper replaces, partitions the corpus's kills into temporal episodes (before the spatial split) almost
identically to the derived value: ARI 0.9225. The constant changes; the episodes it produces change
little. For this step, the derived constant justifies the conference version's partition rather than
overturning it.

%   gaps tested 6..60 s; temporal.plateau_s [10.0, 18.0] (threshold spec_pooled.json plateau_ari 0.9)
% note: for patch 15.14 alone the plateau starts at 12 s (ari_vs_valley "10" = 0.8991, FB/details_15.14.json)

Two qualifications follow from the same analysis. First, the valley is a mid-game feature. In the
pilot, a clear dip appears only between 10 and 20 minutes (valley 12.2\,s, depth 0.94); before
10 minutes the dip is too shallow to pass the acceptance threshold (depth 0.96 against 0.95), and
after 20 minutes the interval density has no dip at all. In the late game $G$ is therefore a convention
justified only by the plateau.
% src: FEAT/temporal_boundary/temporal_boundary.json by_phase: 0-10 min depth 0.9588 ok=false; 10-20 min valley 12.1619 depth 0.9400 ok=true; 20-30 and 30+ min valley NaN depth 1.0; threshold MAX_VALLEY_DEPTH = 0.95 in analysis/temporal_boundary.py
Second, Mehrzadi and Feitelson warn that using activity gaps both to place boundaries and to judge
them is circular \cite{mehrzadi2012session}. The spatial boundary is therefore validated against an
independent signal.

\subsection{The spatial boundary \texorpdfstring{$D$}{D}}

Kills that are close in time can still belong to different fights, for instance simultaneous fights in
two lanes. An episode is therefore split so that no two of its kills are more than $D$ apart: taken in
time order, each kill joins the first group whose members all lie within $D$ of it, or starts a new
group (gameplay/fight\_clustering.py, \texttt{split\_kill\_cluster\_spatial}).

$D$ is estimated from a signal the temporal rule does not use: whether two consecutive kills involve
the same champion, as killer, victim or assister. A champion cannot be in two places at once, so among
pairs no more than $\hat G$ apart, the rate of sharing a champion as a function of distance traces where
``same fight'' ends. $D$ is the distance at which that rate falls to 0.5, the decision point that
misclassifies the fewest pairs when both errors cost the same. Over the 5,460,008 consecutive pairs
within $\hat G$, $\hat D = 4{,}263.87$\,u (match bootstrap 95\% CI 4,256--4,274\,u). In every map region
the crossing lies between 3,974\,u (top lane) and 4,412\,u (bases), within 7\% of the pooled value, so a
single $D$ serves the whole map.

% src: FB/details_pooled.json spatial.by_region crossover_u top 3974.5, mid 4180.8, bot 4308.2, river 4279.0, jungle_top 4288.2,

Pairs that the rule links (within $\hat G$ and $\hat D$; 4,622,011 pairs) share a champion in 93.8\%

\emph{Reported precision.} The sharing rate is computed in distance bins, and $\hat D$ is a linear
interpolation between the centres of the two bins that straddle 0.5: 3,750\,u (rate 0.647) and
4,500\,u (rate 0.432), which are 750\,u apart. The bootstrap resamples matches but keeps the bins and
the interpolation fixed. Its 18\,u interval therefore measures the sampling variation of the two bin
means; it cannot see the error of assuming that the rate is linear across a 750\,u gap. We do not claim
precision finer than that gap. Only 1.7\% of in-window pairs lie within $\pm$10\% of $\hat D$ (between
3,837 and 4,690\,u), so a change of $D$ of that size relabels at most 1.7\% of pairs. The detector was
run with $D = 4{,}264.0$\,u, the estimate $\hat D = 4{,}263.87$\,u rounded before it was injected.

\subsection{Presence gate, lead and anchor: \texorpdfstring{$R$, $B$ and $M$}{R, B and M}}

A kill alone does not show that both teams were in a position to fight shortly before it. An episode
therefore becomes an \emph{engagement} only when, at the cutoff
$\tau$ placed $B$ before its first kill, at least $M = 2$ alive champions of each team stand within $R$
of the first kill's position, the \emph{anchor}. ``Alive'' is read from the last one-minute frame before
$\tau$. Positions are read from a 5\,s grid interpolated between one-minute frames, with each kill
participant's path drawn towards the position of its kill (gameplay/fights.py,
\texttt{\_validate\_teamfight\_at\_engage}). Ke et al.\ require at least two players on one
side~\cite{ke2022}; we require two on both sides.

Neither $R$ nor $B$ can be estimated from Match-V5, which records positions once a minute. We therefore
hold both fixed, by assumption, at values that the game itself uses for a closely related purpose.
$R = 1{,}600$\,u is the radius within which enemy champions share the experience granted by a champion's
death. $B = 15$\,s is the window within which the game credits a champion with an assist on Summoner's
Rift~\cite{lolwiki2026experience,lolwiki2026assist}.

%   lolwiki2026assist (Assist, revision 4016680: 15 s Summoner's Rift, 20 s Howling Abyss) -- rule_constants_evidence.md section 1
% src: SHARDS/manifest.json detector.TF2_VALIDITY_RADIUS 1600.0, TF2_ENGAGE_PRE_KILL_MS 15000; FB/spec_pooled.json

\subsection{Which events the label may read}\label{sec:def-attribution}

The outcome label (\texttt{market\_event}, Section~\fwdref{sec:label}) reads events in the window
$[\tau,\ \max(t_{\mathrm{last\,kill}} + 1\,\mathrm{ms},\ \tau + H))$ with $H = 35$\,s, cut short if an
ace occurs inside it.
% src: gameplay/fights.py label_window_end_ts(); _truncate_fights_at_ace(); SHARDS/manifest.json detector.FIGHT_HORIZON_SEC 35
A kill or a structure on the other side of the map should not decide a fight, so within that window
label attribution keeps only events whose position lies inside a \emph{disc of radius $D$ centred on the
anchor}, that is, a disc of diameter $2D = 8{,}528$\,u. Events without a position, such as ward kills,
are dropped.
% src: core/presets.py PRESETS["v3.3"] LABEL_EVENT_ATTRIBUTION "engagement"; gameplay/labels.py attribute_events():
%   radius = LABEL_ATTRIBUTION_RADIUS_U (0.0 in core/config.py) -> CLUSTER_MAX_DIAMETER (4264.0 in v3.3); keeps events with
%   (dx^2 + dy^2) <= radius^2; anchor = centroid_x/y = first kill position (data/index_split.py _fight_anchor,
%   gameplay/fight_clustering.py finalize_kill_cluster)
We use radius $D$, not $D/2$, because the spatial split guarantees that every kill of an unmerged
episode lies within $D$ of its first kill. Radius $D$ is thus the smallest anchor-centred radius that
cannot exclude any of the episode's own kills, whereas a disc of diameter $D$ could. The price is that
the disc can also admit a kill, structure or monster up to $D$ from the anchor that belongs to a
neighbouring fight. After the re-engagement merge (Table~\ref{tab:constants}), the merged engagement
keeps the earlier anchor, so kills of the absorbed episode can lie up to $D + 2{,}000$\,u from it,
outside the disc.

\subsection{Re-estimation per patch}

Patches change the rules and parameters of an esport title, so the validity of a model built on one
version is not guaranteed on the next \cite{chitayat2023beyond}. The constants are therefore not fixed
once.
% src: D:/LOL_Project/references/PedrassoliChitayat2023_BeyondTheMeta_AIIDE.pdf (text extracted 2026-09-14): introduction and
%   related work (patches alter game rules and parameters; with one-hot character encodings "model validity is no longer
%   guaranteed" as parameters change).  Paraphrase of the paper's motivation only: its own experiment finds no clear drop on the
%   held-out patches 7.32 / 7.33 (NN1 no difference, NN2 a small drop within the margin of error, NN3 consistent), so the
%   sentence claims no measured decay.
A pipeline re-estimates $G$ and $D$ for each patch and applies a drift rule fixed before it was run: a patch keeps the pooled definition if its
$G$ lies inside the pooled plateau and its $D$ lies within 10\% of the pooled value with an overlapping
interval.
% src: FB/drift.json diameter_rel_tol 0.1, plateau_ari 0.9; analysis/fight_boundary_pipeline.py DIAMETER_REL_TOL = 0.10;
%   docs/DEFINITION_EVIDENCE.md section 10 (rule fixed before running)
$R$ and $B$ are not estimated; for them the check is the patch-note search described above. For patches
15.14--15.16 the per-patch values are $G = 14.0$, 13.5 and 13.7\,s and $D = 4{,}285$, 4,265 and
4,241\,u (at most 0.53\% from the pooled value), all inside, so one definition serves the corpus. The
value of $G$ for 15.16 equals the pooled value to every digit because both fall on the same grid node.
% src: FB/drift.json verdict "pooled"; gap_s 13.9637 / 13.4896 / 13.7246; diameter_u 4285.26 / 4265.27 / 4241.47; diameter_rel_diff max 0.00525
A pilot on 3,000 sampled matches per patch had placed patch 15.15 at 12.6\,s and 4,146\,u; on the full
corpus that difference vanishes. The tolerances of the drift rule are therefore wide relative to the
between-patch variation actually observed. Zaliapin and Ben-Zion likewise defend their declustering by
reporting how little their cluster statistics move under wide changes of catalogue parameters
\cite{zaliapin2022perspectives}.
% src: FB/sample_3000/drift.json patches."15.15".gap_s 12.5893, diameter_u 4146.02

\subsection{Scale classes}

Scale is a post-hoc attribute and is used only for reporting, never as a model input. The participation
count of each side is the number of its champions among the killers, victims and assisters of the
engagement's kills, plus the actors of other timeline events placed within 3,000\,u of the anchor
between $\tau$ and the last kill. Structure and monster events are excluded from this count, and events
without a position are placed at the actor's interpolated position.
% src: gameplay/fights.py _collect_interactions_in_radius() (TF2_INTERACTION_RADIUS 3000.0; ELITE_MONSTER_KILL, BUILDING_KILL,

%   TF2_EXCLUDE_SHOP_INTERACTIONS False); det_cluster_blue/red -> scripts/build_corpus_shard.py cluster_blue/red.
% note: _merge_adjacent_candidates() does not update det_cluster_blue/red, so a merged engagement keeps the earlier candidate's counts.
Classes use the smaller side's count, $n_{\min}$. Among the 532,547 engagements of the v3.3 corpus,
$n_{\min}$ is 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 in 19.1, 40.2, 20.0, 13.4 and 7.2\% of engagements (42 engagements, 0.01\%,
carry no participation record). The smaller side's count alone therefore has no antimode. The diagonal
of the joint distribution does: 2v2 (14.7\%) is a mode, 3v3 is 7.6\%, 4v4 (6.2\%) is a trough, and 5v5
(7.2\%) is a second mode at the edge of the range.

\subsection{What a kill-anchored definition cannot see}\label{sec:def-killless}

Fights in which no champion dies are outside the population by construction. Two questions follow:
how common such fights are, and whether their outcome could be scored if they were detected.

\emph{Frequency.} A kill-anchored detector cannot count what it omits, so we counted proximity
directly. Schubert et al.\ report that 81.1\% of the proximity encounters they detected in Dota~2
contained no kill, and they restricted outcome analysis to encounters that one side won on
kills~\cite{schubert2016encounter}.

Three cautions govern these numbers. Their denominator is proximity encounters, not engagements.
Proximity is not commitment: champions near one another need not be fighting, so the per-match rates are
not a count of the fights the corpus omits and cannot be divided into engagement counts. And neither
track observes a fight between frames: the frame-only track places every champion on a straight line
between positions a minute apart, and on both tracks a champion counts as alive if it was alive at the
last frame, so neither share bounds the omitted fights in either direction.

\emph{Measurability.} Detection is only half of the problem, because a fight can be won without a kill.
Even if a kill-less fight were found, the label could not score it. The label's window starts at $\tau$,
which is defined from the first kill, and its attribution disc is centred on the first kill's position;
without a kill, neither exists. Imposing a window and a centre would not solve this. The label's
currency is kill gold plus priced team gold for structures, turret plates and epic monsters. A fight
that trades health, cooldowns and position but ends without a death, a structure or a monster has no
priced event, so its swing is zero and falls inside the 300\,g dead zone. Refinement would then fall back
on survivor counts read from one-minute frames and on structure events, neither of which describes the
exchange.

With this telemetry the outcome of a kill-less fight is therefore unmeasurable, not merely undetected: the
timeline records health and damage only once a minute, and most kill-less encounters contain nothing that
the label prices. A kill-less fight that forces a retreat and concedes a structure or a monster would be
priced, but through the objective rather than through the fight.

Kills alone do not settle outcomes either. Among 25,995 fights detected under the conference-version
constants on 5,000 matches, the side that won the kills and the side that won the structures were
opposite in 7.54\% of fights (1,959). A rule that ranks kills above structures, or structures above
kills, decides those fights by fiat. The market label instead adds both into one gold swing, so neither
is ranked above the other unless the swing falls inside the dead zone. Measuring kill-less fights would
require damage or health telemetry at sub-minute resolution, which the public API does not provide
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Validity summary}

Following Jacobs and Wallach \cite{jacobs2021measurement}, the evidence falls into five parts.
\emph{Content validity}: the rule combines the kill requirement of Ke et al.\ with a proximity condition in
the manner of Schubert et al., and states what it excludes. \emph{Convergent validity}: the seismological proximity computed on the same kind of
pairs has the same bimodal structure. \emph{Discriminant validity}: among consecutive kills within
$\hat G$, pairs the rule links share a champion in 93.8\% of cases, against 5.5\% for pairs it
separates. \emph{Test--retest reliability}: three patches fall inside one definition, supported by
bootstrap intervals and the ARI plateau. \emph{Predictive validity}: with every input restricted to what
is observable at the cutoff, the outcome of the 532,547 engagements in 191,940 matches is predictable at
a match-grouped out-of-fold AUC of 0.670.
% src: FEAT/scale_decomposition_v33_market_event.json n 532547, n_matches 191940, overall_auc 0.6699089924634439
Our analyses after the conference version had concluded that predictability grows with engagement
scale; that conclusion is withdrawn. It was carried by a time feature normalised by each match's own
length, which is not known at the cutoff, and with that input removed the sign of the difference between
classes follows the arbitrary teamfight cut. The conference paper reported no scale classes, but its
pipeline used the same time feature, together with map anchors that included structures and objectives
taken later in the match.
