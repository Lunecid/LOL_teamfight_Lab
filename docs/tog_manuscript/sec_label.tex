\section{Engagement Outcome Label}\label{sec:label}

The prediction target is which team won an engagement. This section explains why that needs a
definition (Section~\ref{sec:label-why}), defines the label step by step
(Section~\ref{sec:label-def}), shows where its prices come from and where they depart from the game's
rules (Section~\ref{sec:label-prices}), lists the choices that remain ours
(Section~\ref{sec:label-choices}), and tests whether any conclusion depends on them
(Section~\ref{sec:label-family}).

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Why the winner needs a definition}\label{sec:label-why}

An engagement rarely ends with one side simply wiped out. A team can lose two champions and destroy a
turret; it can trade one kill for one and take a dragon; it can win every kill while the other team
destroys a turret nearby. Any rule that names a winner must decide how a kill compares with a turret,
an assist or a dragon, and those relative values are the label's weights. The conference version set
them by hand: its label combined kills, shutdowns, kill streaks, assists, bounties, objectives and
lane position through fourteen weights, a temperature, and hand-set tables of objective tiers, lane
priorities and special-kill bonuses, all chosen by us, and a reviewer rightly asked whether a model
trained on such a label learns the game or the heuristic.

The game already puts a price on most of these events. It pays gold for kills, assists, turret
plates, turrets and epic monsters, and gold buys the items that make a champion
stronger~\cite{lolwiki2026gold} (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Gold is therefore the currency in which the
game itself converts a won exchange into future fighting strength. Our label adds up the gold each
team received from the events of the engagement and names the team that received more, so the
exchange rate between a kill and a turret is the game's, not ours. Two things in particular keep the
label from being free of our choices. First, in the match timelines of our corpus, which come from
Riot's timeline endpoint~\cite{riotapi2024timeline}, each kill event carries bounty and shutdown-gold
fields, but plate events carry no bounty field, and the bounty of a structure or epic-monster event is
non-zero in only 3.0\% and 0.9\% of cases (300 sampled matches). Those prices must therefore be
estimated (Section~\ref{sec:label-prices}). The estimate is fitted to how team gold moves, not to how
well any model predicts the label, so it cannot have been tuned towards an easier target. Second, gold
does not price everything a fight wins: a drake's permanent buff for the killing
team~\cite{lolwiki2026dragonslayer}, the timed Baron Nashor and Elder Dragon
buffs~\cite{lolwiki2026baron,lolwiki2026elderdragon}, experience and the time an enemy spends dead carry
no gold. The label measures the material exchange
as the game pays it, not its consequence for the match; Section~\ref{sec:label-value} points to where
that consequence is measured separately.

\subsection{The label, step by step}\label{sec:label-def}

The label is computed for each engagement from its cutoff $\tau$, 15\,s before the first kill at
time $t_{\mathrm{F}}$; from its anchor $\mathbf{a}$, the position of that first kill; and from its
last kill at time $t_{\mathrm{L}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:definition}). It is 1 if the blue team won and 0
if the red team won.
% src: SHARDS/manifest.json detector.TF2_ENGAGE_PRE_KILL_MS 15000; anchor = first kill position
%   (REPO/data/index_split.py _fight_anchor); y = 1 for team 100 (REPO/gameplay/labels.py _label_event_team_sign, sign +1).

\emph{Step 1: the window.} The label reads events in
\begin{align}
W &= [\tau,\ t_{\mathrm{end}}), \label{eq:label-window}\\
t_{\mathrm{end}} &= \min\{\max(t_{\mathrm{L}} + 1\,\mathrm{ms},\ \tau + H),\ t_{\mathrm{A}} + 1\,\mathrm{ms}\}, \nonumber
\end{align}
where $H = 35$\,s and $t_{\mathrm{A}}$ is the first ace at or after $\tau$ ($t_{\mathrm{A}} = \infty$
if there is none). Unless an ace comes first, the window contains the engagement's last kill and
reaches at least 20\,s past its first kill, the reach of the conference version, whose cutoff lay
10\,s before the first kill with a 30\,s horizon. An ace ends the window at once, because the losing
side then has no champion left; an ace before the last kill therefore also excludes the kills that
follow it. In one further case, not counted, the window is cut short: when it overlaps a later,
higher-priority engagement whose anchor lies more than $D$ away, it ends at that engagement's cutoff
(Table~\ref{tab:constants}).

\emph{Step 2: which events belong to the engagement.} A kill or a turret on the other side of the map
should not decide this fight, so the label keeps only the events of $W$ whose position $\mathbf{p}_e$
lies in a disc around the anchor:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-attr}
\mathcal{E} = \{\, e : t_e \in W,\ \lVert \mathbf{p}_e - \mathbf{a} \rVert \le D \,\}.
\end{equation}
The disc has radius $D = 4{,}264$\,u, the detector's cluster diameter, and therefore a diameter of
$2D = 8{,}528$\,u; Section~\ref{sec:def-attribution} explains why the radius is $D$ and not $D/2$.
Events without a position, such as ward kills, are dropped.

%   REPO/gameplay/labels.py attribute_events() keeps (dx^2 + dy^2) <= radius^2 and skips events without a position.

\emph{Step 3: who gained, and how much.} Each event $e \in \mathcal{E}$ has a side $s(e)$, $+1$ if
it benefits blue and $-1$ if it benefits red: the killer's team for a champion kill, the killing team
for an epic monster, and the team opposite the owner for a plate or a structure. An event credited to
no team, such as a champion executed by a turret, has $s(e) = 0$. Calling plates, structures and epic
monsters \emph{objective events}, the gold of an event is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-gold}
g(e) = \begin{cases}
b_e + h_e + \pi_{\mathrm{k}} + \pi_{\mathrm{a}}\,|A_e| & \text{kill},\\
\pi_{c(e)} & \text{objective},\\
0 & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $b_e$ and $h_e$ are the bounty and shutdown gold recorded on the kill event, $A_e$ is its set of
assisting champions, and the prices $\pi$ come from the table of Section~\ref{sec:label-prices}:
$\pi_{\mathrm{k}} = 20$\,g per kill and $\pi_{\mathrm{a}} = 45$\,g per assisting champion on top of the
recorded bounty, and one price $\pi_{c(e)}$ for each plate, turret tier, inhibitor and epic monster
type $c(e)$. The \emph{gold swing} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-swing}
\Delta = \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}} s(e)\, g(e),
\end{equation}
positive when blue received more gold.

\emph{Step 4: the verdict.} A swing larger than one base kill bounty names the winner; a smaller one
is treated as materially even:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-verdict}
y = \begin{cases}
1 & \Delta > \varepsilon,\\
0 & \Delta < -\varepsilon,\\
r & |\Delta| \le \varepsilon,
\end{cases}
\qquad \varepsilon = 300\,\mathrm{g}.
\end{equation}
The dead zone $\varepsilon$ is the bounty of a champion at levels 1--6~\cite{lolwiki2026bounties,riot2025patch2509}.
With the fitted prices, one unanswered kill at that bounty is worth at least 320\,g, enough on its
own to decide, whereas a single plate, 120\,g, is not.

%   base bounty 300 g at levels 1-6, set in 25.09, no change listed in the 25.10-25.16 notes (references_audit.md N4).
% cmd: 300 + 20 (per-kill price) + 45 x |A| >= 320 > 300;  plate price 120 < 300 (event_prices.json pooled.plates 120.0).

\emph{Step 5: refinement inside the dead zone.} When $|\Delta| \le \varepsilon$, three discrete facts
are consulted in a fixed order, and the first that is not zero decides:
\begin{align}
K &= \textstyle\sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{kill}},\ t_{\mathrm{F}} \le t_e \le t_{\mathrm{L}}} s(e), \nonumber\\
N &= n^{\mathrm{blue}}_{\mathrm{alive}} - n^{\mathrm{red}}_{\mathrm{alive}}, \label{eq:label-refine}\\
S &= \textstyle\sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obj}}} s(e), \nonumber
\end{align}
and $r = 1$ if that fact is positive and $r = 0$ if it is negative. $K$ is the net number of kills
inside the disc timestamped between the engagement's first and last kill. These are normally the
engagement's own kills, but $K$ can include a neighbouring fight's kills in that interval and miss
kills of a merged episode that lie outside the disc (Section~\ref{sec:def-attribution}). $N$ is the difference in
living champions, counted over all five champions of each team in the last one-minute frame at or
before the last kill; because frames are held, this count can be up to a minute old. $S$ is the net
number of plates, structures and epic monsters taken inside the disc during the window. If $K$, $N$
and $S$ are all zero the engagement is a draw, and draws are dropped: 33,905 of the 566,452 detected
engagements (5.99\%), leaving 532,547 labelled engagements from 191,940 matches, of which blue won
50.8\%. Of the 532,547 labelled engagements, the gold swing decides 85.99\% (95\% interval 85.90 to
86.09), $K$ decides 9.28\% (9.20 to 9.35), $N$ 3.51\% (3.46 to 3.56) and $S$ 1.22\% (1.19 to 1.25); the
draws are 5.99\% (5.92 to 6.05) of the 566,452 detected engagements. The refinement decides 14.7\%, 14.3\%
and 12.4\% of labelled picks, skirmishes and teamfights (scale classes of Section~\ref{sec:definition},
teamfights from $n_{\min} = 4$), and 13.7\%, 16.3\%, 11.8\% and 10.1\% of labelled engagements whose cutoff
falls in minutes 2--10, 10--20, 20--30 and after 30. Draws are most common early: 9.7\% of detected
engagements with a cutoff in minutes 2--10, against 2.3\% to 4.0\% later. The intervals come from 2,000
bootstrap resamples of the 194,676 matches that hold a detected engagement, and none of the grouped
intervals extends more than 0.41 percentage points from its estimate.

\emph{A worked example.} In an engagement's window and disc, blue kills two red champions: one with
a 300\,g bounty and two assists ($300 + 20 + 2 \times 45 = 410$\,g), one with a 300\,g bounty,
150\,g of shutdown gold and one assist ($300 + 150 + 20 + 45 = 515$\,g). Red kills one blue champion
with a 300\,g bounty and three assists (455\,g), and blue destroys a plate (120\,g). The swing is
$410 + 515 + 120 - 455 = 590$\,g, beyond the dead zone, so blue wins. Had each team instead killed one
champion with a 300\,g bounty, blue's kill with three assists (455\,g) and red's with one (365\,g),
and had red destroyed a plate, the swing would be $455 - 365 - 120 = -30$\,g, inside the dead zone.
The kills cancel ($K = 0$); if both teams had the same number of champions alive in the last frame
($N = 0$), red's plate gives $S = -1$, and red wins.

\subsection{Where the prices come from}\label{sec:label-prices}

Plates, structures and monsters carry no usable gold amount in the timeline, but the frames carry
each player's total gold once a minute (Table~\ref{tab:terms}). Suppose that in one minute a team
destroys a turret, kills a champion and farms twenty minions: its gold rises by what each of these
paid, plus passive income. Across a million minutes the events occur in different combinations, so
their separate contributions can be recovered by regression. Ordinary least squares (OLS) finds the
per-event amounts that best reproduce the observed gold changes. For every team $j$ in every
frame interval,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-prices}
\Delta G_j = \alpha + \beta_{\mathrm{b}} B_j + \sum_{k} \beta_k\, n_{jk}
 + \gamma_{\mathrm{L}}\, \Delta C^{\mathrm{L}}_j + \gamma_{\mathrm{J}}\, \Delta C^{\mathrm{J}}_j + u_j,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta G_j$ is the change in the team's total gold, $B_j$ the bounty and shutdown gold
recorded on the team's kills, $n_{jk}$ the team's count of events of type $k$ (kills, assists,
plates, turrets by tier, inhibitors, a first-turret indicator, the six epic monster types and ward
kills), and $\Delta C^{\mathrm{L}}_j$ and $\Delta C^{\mathrm{J}}_j$ the team's changes in lane and
jungle creep score. Intervals that start before 2:00 are dropped. We sampled 8,000 matches from each patch (24,000), which give 1,173,822 team-intervals.

A coefficient is the average team gold associated with one more event of its type when the other
counts are held fixed. It is not the payout written in the rules, and it absorbs any income that
tends to arrive in the same minute as the event. The fit explains 96.2\% of the variance of the gold
changes pooled over patches, and 96.2\% within each patch. The OLS standard errors treat the
intervals as independent, which minutes of the same match are not, so we also refit the regression
on 200 bootstrap samples that resample whole matches~\cite{efron1993bootstrap} and report the
2.5th and 97.5th percentiles as a 95\% interval.

The label reads a rounded table built from the pooled fit (Table~\ref{tab:prices}). Kill gold is
read from the event at face value, and the kill and assist coefficients are added on top of it.
Each epic monster's coefficient is increased by the jungle creep-score coefficient (23.33\,g), because
the table construction assumes that the frames count an epic monster as one jungle creep, whose gold
the creep-score term would otherwise absorb; a negative result is set to zero. The first-turret term
is left out. Prices are rounded to 5\,g, and an event type needs at least 200 events to be priced.

\subsection{Choices that remain ours}\label{sec:label-choices}

Reading the game's economy removes the weights between event types, but these choices remain.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Window.} $H = 35$\,s and the stop at an ace (Eq.~\eqref{eq:label-window}). In a 553-match
  pilot with the minute-frame \texttt{market\_lex} label, verdicts with 45\,s and 60\,s windows agreed
  with those with 35\,s on 97.5\% and 94.3\% of the engagements labelled at both horizons
  (Table~\ref{tab:constants}); the \texttt{market\_event} label has not been re-run at other horizons.
  % src: FEAT/prediction_situation_pilot.json n_matches 553; v3.LABEL_TYPE "market_lex"; checks.horizon."45"
  %   label_agreement_with_35 0.97475, n_common_with_35 1307; checks.horizon."60" label_agreement_with_35 0.94313,
  %   n_common_with_35 1266 (as printed in sec_definition.tex Table tab:constants row "Label horizon").
  %   No market_event horizon check exists in REV (A2 label family varies no horizon).
\item \emph{Attribution.} A disc of radius $D$ centred on the first kill, fixed in place and identical
  in every direction; events without a position never count (Eq.~\eqref{eq:label-attr}). The disc can
  admit the kill, structure or monster of a neighbouring fight and can miss kills of a merged episode
  that lie outside it. The swing counts every kill inside the disc in the window, including kills
  before the first or after the last kill of the engagement, while $K$ counts only the kills inside
  the disc timestamped between the first and the last kill.
  % src: REPO/gameplay/labels.py _compute_label_market_event() sums over all attributed events of the window;
  %   _lex_refine() restricts kills to [first_kill_ts, last_kill_ts] among the attributed events;
  %   sec_definition.tex sec:def-attribution (neighbouring fight; merged episode up to D + 2,000 u).
\item \emph{Dead zone.} $\varepsilon = 300$\,g, one base bounty.
\item \emph{Refinement order.} Kills, then living champions, then structures. The living-champion
  count covers all ten champions and is read from the last frame before the last kill.
\item \emph{Draws.} Dropped, not split or assigned at random.
\item \emph{Price estimation.} A linear model with a constant passive income per interval, the
  regressor set of Eq.~\eqref{eq:label-prices}, intervals from 2:00, patches pooled, prices rounded
  to 5\,g, the one-creep add-back and the zero floor for monsters, the first-turret term omitted, and
  the bounty read at face value rather than at its coefficient. The prices are fitted on matches from
  the same corpus that is later evaluated (Section~\ref{subsec:limitations}).
\item \emph{Gold the label does not read.} Bounty fields on structure and monster events, non-zero on
  3.0\% of structure kills and 0.9\% of epic-monster kills in 300 sampled matches, and every reward that
  is not gold: buffs, the Dragon Soul, experience and death timers.
  % cmd: seed-0, 300-match cache sample above: 98 / 3,236 = 3.03 %; 22 / 2,521 = 0.87 %.
  % src: REPO/gameplay/labels.py _priced_event_gold() reads table prices only (no event bounty for non-kill events);
  %   DRAGON_SOUL_GIVEN and CHAMPION_SPECIAL_KILL priced 0 g.
\end{enumerate}
The label also cannot credit a fight won without any priced event: a kill-less exchange that ends in a
retreat has a swing of zero and is not an engagement in the first place
(Sections~\ref{sec:def-killless} and~\ref{subsec:limitations}).

\subsection{Does any conclusion depend on those choices?}\label{sec:label-family}

If a model learned the arithmetic of one label rather than the state of the game, changing the
arithmetic would change what it can predict. A reviewer of the conference version raised this concern
and asked for alternative labels, naming raw kill advantage and learned weights, and for the
sensitivity of the label to its weights. The price table is our learned-weight label, learned from the gold record rather than from
the prediction target. Weights could instead be learned from outcomes, and we chose not to do so for
two reasons. Weights chosen to make the engagement label easier to predict would select the label by
its predictability, the very circularity the reviewer's question guards against. Weights fitted to the
match result would turn the label into a measure of match value, which depends on the state of the
match before the engagement, state that the model's inputs already describe; we keep match value as a
separate yardstick instead (Section~\ref{sec:label-value}). Against the price table we set eleven

Each label drops its own draws, so the variants label different engagements, and an AUC difference
between two of them would mix the effect of the label with that of the population. We therefore score
all twelve labels on the 484,474 engagements (188,104 matches) that every one of them labels. On these
common rows each label gets its own model under the headline protocol
(Section~\fwdref{sec:prediction}): LightGBM gradient-boosted trees~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} (400 trees,
learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves) on the same 6,164 input columns, five folds that keep each match within
one fold and are identical for every label, and out-of-fold predictions, so that every engagement is
scored by a model that never saw its match. The score is the area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve (AUC): the probability that an engagement blue won receives a higher predicted
probability of a blue win than one red won, 0.5 for guessing and 1 for a perfect ranking. Intervals
come from 1,000 bootstrap samples of matches, and every label is scored on the same samples, so
differences between labels are paired. \emph{Agreement} is the share of engagements on which a variant
and \texttt{market\_event} name the same winner, among those both label; the \emph{flip rate} is its
complement. To test directly whether a model learns a label's arithmetic, we keep the
\texttt{market\_event} model's out-of-fold predictions fixed and score them against each variant's
labels (\emph{transfer}). A model that had fitted the arithmetic of \texttt{market\_event} should rank
a variant's winners worse than that variant's own model does.

\subsection{Gold is not value}\label{sec:label-value}

The label records which team took more gold, not whether that gold changed who is likely to win the
match. A kill that raises a team's chance of winning is worth more than one that does not, the
distinction behind Maymin's smart kills and worthless deaths~\cite{maymin2021smart}. We therefore keep
match value as a separate yardstick. Section~\fwdref{sec:limit} scores each engagement by the change in
an independently fitted model's win probability from just before the cutoff to the engagement's last
kill. On 25,177 held-out engagements, the realised \texttt{market\_event} outcome alone accounts for
55.0\% of the variance of that change and gives its direction for 92.4\% of engagements, both weighted
so that every match counts equally. The binary gold verdict thus accounts for a little over half of the variation in what
an engagement does to the match's win probability; Section~\fwdref{sec:limit} examines the rest.
